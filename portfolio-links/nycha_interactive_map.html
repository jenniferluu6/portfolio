Built in a Generation

New York put up most of its public housing in twenty years, and it is wearing out on the same schedule. NYCHA's repair need now runs to $78.3 billion over twenty years, and nearly half of it, $37.6 billion, is needed immediately. The bill is front-loaded because the buildings are: roofs, boilers, elevators and risers installed in the same twenty-year window reach the end of their service lives in the same twenty-year window.

The spike that set the schedule

This curve is also the repair curve, shifted about sixty years, and New York is standing in the middle of it. That is why 74% of the assessed need sits in apartments, heating, building exteriors and plumbing. The systems, not the structures.

There is no appropriation coming for $78 billion

Federal money for construction peaked in the 1960s and was effectively gone by the 1970s. So the response has not been to fund the repairs directly. It has been to change how the buildings are financed, and residents are deciding that themselves, development by development, choosing between staying in Section 9, converting through PACT, or joining the Preservation Trust.

PACT has reached roughly 31,500 apartments against a 62,000 unit target. Whichever way a development votes, the clock the buildings are on does not change.
